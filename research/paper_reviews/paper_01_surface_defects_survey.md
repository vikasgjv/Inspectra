# Deep Learning for Automatic Vision-Based Recognition of Industrial Surface Defects: A Survey

## Basic Information

- **Authors:** Michela Prunella, Roberto Maria Scardigno, Domenico Buongiorno, Antonio Brunetti, Nicola Longo, Raffaele Carli, Mariagrazia Dotoli, and Vitoantonio Bevilacqua
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** IEEE Access
- **Type:** Survey / Review Paper
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3271748

## Problem Statement

Industrial quality inspection requires accurate identification of surface defects. Traditional machine learning methods often depend on manually designed features and expert knowledge.

The paper reviews how deep learning can be used to automate the recognition of industrial surface defects.

## Objective

The objective of this paper is to provide a comprehensive review of deep learning-based industrial surface defect recognition systems.

The survey analyzes more than 220 research articles and studies different defect recognition tasks and learning approaches.

## Methodology

The paper follows a systematic literature review approach based on PRISMA guidelines.

The reviewed approaches are classified using a two-dimensional taxonomy based on:

- Defect recognition task
  - Classification
  - Detection
  - Segmentation

- Learning paradigm
  - Fully supervised
  - Weakly supervised
  - Semi-supervised
  - Unsupervised

The survey also discusses different deep learning architectures and techniques such as:

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)
- Autoencoders
- Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
- Transfer learning
- Attention mechanisms
- Explainable AI

## Dataset

Since this is a survey paper, it does not use one specific dataset.

The reviewed studies use different types of industrial data, including:

- RGB images
- Grayscale images
- X-ray images
- Thermal and infrared images
- CT scans

## Key Findings

The survey found that CNN-based methods are widely used for industrial surface defect recognition because they can learn both detailed and higher-level patterns.

The main tasks used in industrial inspection are:

- Classification
- Object detection
- Segmentation

The paper also highlights increasing interest in:

- Transfer learning
- Data augmentation
- Unsupervised learning
- Defect localization
- Explainable AI

## Challenges / Limitations

The paper identifies several important challenges:

- Limited availability of high-quality labeled datasets
- Class imbalance
- Detection of subtle and multi-scale defects
- Difficulty in recognizing previously unseen defects
- Need for better generalization across industrial environments
- Lack of public datasets for benchmarking

## Research Gap

Existing approaches still require improvement in:

- Flexible inspection systems
- Time-efficient solutions
- Explainable AI
- Robust validation across different datasets and domains
- Public datasets for fair benchmarking

## Relevance to Inspectra

This paper provides the overall research foundation for Inspectra.

It helps us understand the different approaches available for automated industrial inspection, including classification, detection, segmentation, supervised learning, and unsupervised learning.

It also highlights important challenges such as limited datasets, generalization, and explainability that can help us identify a suitable research direction for Inspectra.
